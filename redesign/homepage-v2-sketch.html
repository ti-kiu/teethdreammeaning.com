Teeth Dream Meaning

Classical dream interpretation, read from the source books — not from other websites.

Start here: teeth falling out

Woke up from a teeth falling out dream? The plainest reading: something in your life feels like it's slipping out of control — money going out faster than it should, a family member you're quietly worried about, or a situation you're trying to hold onto that won't hold. The classical books land on that reading again and again.

Teeth falling out — by scenario

Does it mean someone will die?
Only under stated conditions — with emigration, long sickness and thirty-dinars alternatives in the same entries.
Ibn Sirin pp. 349–352 · Al-Nabulsi shamela 177
Teeth falling out with blood
Blood spoils the project, flips a caught tooth to severed ties — a debt long before a death omen.
Ibn Sirin “Teeth” + “Toothbrush” · Al-Nabulsi shamela 177
Falling into your hand, lap, or the ground
Hand = money, lap = a son, ground = death — the three-surface rule, both rule-sets quoted.
Al-Nabulsi shamela 177
One tooth or the whole mouth?
One tooth pays one creditor; the whole mouth ends a household — the counting rules.
Ibn Sirin “Molar Tooth” p. 281 · Al-Nabulsi shamela 177
Bad omen or good sign?
Both verdicts on the same pages: longevity and dirhams by count — death only if the tooth is lost or buried.
Ibn Sirin front matter + “Teeth” · Al-Nabulsi exceptions
Is it caused by stress?
The books never call it a stress dream; the study that measured distress found r = 0.02 — no link.
Frontiers in Psychology 9:1812 (2018) · Yu, Dreaming 22:1 (2012)
How three traditions read the same dream
The Arabic books count teeth; the Zhou Gong book gives two lines. Same dream, two machines.
Zhou Gong Jie Meng ch. 3 · both Arabic entries

The method, stated plainly

Every page on this site places the two classical dream books' original entries next to each other, each with a checkable location: the English compilation Ibn Seerin's Dictionary of Dreams (Pearl Publishing House, 1992 — a later compilation attributed to Ibn Sirin, not his autograph text), and the Arabic original of Al-Nabulsi's Ta'tir al-Anam (Dar al-Fikr edition), cited to the page in the online library where it can be read directly. Where a Chinese tradition source exists — the Zhou Gong dream book — its lines are quoted from the received text with chapter locations.

Where the books disagree, we show the disagreement instead of smoothing it over. Where one book contradicts itself, we show that too.
