Featured reading

The page the books keep open

One page of Al-Nabulsi's Ta'tir al-Anam carries the most-quoted rule in teeth dreams — and, one paragraph later, its own contradiction. We publish both, because that is what the book actually says.

Ta'tir al-Anam · the tooth entry

Upper teeth falling into the hand: money coming to him — into the lap: a male child — to the ground: the calamity of death.

That is the three-surface rule: hand = money, lap = a son, ground = death. Almost every teeth-dream page on the internet repeats some version of it.

But further down the same page, Al-Nabulsi gives a second rule: teeth falling while he catches them with his hand or in his lap mean his children are cut off and none will be born to him. The tooth that lands in your lap is a son; the tooth you catch ends your line. Both rules are his. Both stand.

Verified at shamela.ws/book/1217/177 (printed page ص180). The full entry, with Ibn Sirin's compilation alongside, is on the teeth falling out page.

Questions people ask at 3 a.m.

Does dreaming about teeth falling out mean something is wrong with my real teeth?

Almost never. The dream is psychological, not dental. That said, if you grind your teeth at night or clench your jaw, your brain may simply be reporting the sensation — the research page covers what the studies measured. A night guard beats a dream journal for that one.

Why are teeth dreams so common?

Teeth are one of the few body parts that visibly change, fall out, and regrow in everyone's life — so the classical books gave them a whole vocabulary: family maps, counting rules, three surfaces. Add nighttime jaw tension and you get the most reported dream on earth.

Is there a difference between teeth falling out and teeth breaking?

Yes, and this dictionary treats them separately. "Falling out" leans toward loss and transition; "breaking" leans toward something worn past its limit. The breaking entry is being written now — the full falling-out answer covers the scenarios that exist today.

Should I be worried if I have this dream again and again?

Recurring teeth dreams usually mean the underlying stressor hasn't been resolved yet, not that anything ominous is coming. The classical books themselves disagree about omens — we show the disagreement instead of smoothing it over. Identifying what feels "loose" in your waking life tends to retire the dream on its own.

The method, stated plainly

Every page on this site places the two classical dream books' original entries next to each other, each with a checkable location: the English compilation Ibn Seerin's Dictionary of Dreams (Pearl Publishing House, 1992 — a later compilation attributed to Ibn Sirin, not his autograph text), and the Arabic original of Al-Nabulsi's Ta'tir al-Anam, cited to the page in the online library where it can be read directly. Where the Zhou Gong tradition has a line for the symbol, it is quoted with its chapter.

Where the books disagree, we show the disagreement instead of smoothing it over. Where one book contradicts itself, we show that too.

This site reports what classical texts say about dream symbols. It does not predict anything and does not give medical, psychological, or religious rulings. The books themselves disagree with each other; no dream book settles what a dream means for the person who had it.
