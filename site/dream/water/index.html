Water Dream Meaning: Fortune, Marriage, or Trial

Water is the single most polysemous symbol in the classical dream books — the longest entry in the Ibn Sirin compilation runs two printed pages. The short version: clear, drinking water reads as life, money, marriage and a son; stagnant, hot, murky or salt water reads as distress, prison, tyranny and sickness. But Al-Nabulsi, while copying most of that, inserts one sentence attributed to Ibn Sirin himself that flips the whole symbol: water in a dream is fitna — a trial of faith. The Chinese Zhou Gong book splits the same way but on different axes. All locations cited below are checkable.

Quick answers, by scene

Did you drink it?

The act of drinking is where the books are kindest. Ibn Sirin: "Drinking a glass of water means protection against any danger from one's enemy and denotes a prosperous year"; drinking more than usual "means longevity." Al-Nabulsi copies the same longevity rule almost verbatim. Zhou Gong: 饮水不休得大财 — "drinking water without stopping: great wealth." Condition of the water decides everything: hot water is severe grief in both Arabic books.

Were you in the water, or under it?

This is the inversion. Standing in water is a trial: Ibn Sirin — "Seeing oneself submerged in a body of water means facing a trial, distress, bewilderment and adversities"; Al-Nabulsi — "whoever sees himself in water is in a trial, affliction and grief." Zhou Gong agrees on immersion but not on standing: 水上行者主大吉 (walking on water: very auspicious) but 自落水中不出凶 (falling in and not getting out: ominous).

Was it stagnant, muddy, or dirty-smelling?

All three Arabic verdicts are negative and specific. Stagnant water = imprisonment and depression; murky water = unlawful earnings, or a tyrant; putrid water = a wretched life. One exception, printed right after: a sick person bathing in murky water and walking out of it recovers; a prisoner bathed in it is released. The filthy water washes only the already-afflicted.

The entries side by side, with locations

Ibn Sirin compilation — "Water" entry

"Water in a dream represents a happy life, money, prosperity, expansion of one's business, increase in one's income, or it could mean marriage. If one sees the water pure and abundant in his dream, it means lowering of prices, peace and social justice. … Drinking a glass of water in a dream means protection against any danger from one's enemy and it denotes a prosperous year. … Drinking more water in a dream than what one usually drinks in wakefulness means longevity. … Stagnant water in a dream means imprisonment, distress, or depression. … Murky waters in a dream represent unlawful earnings. … Murky water in a dream denotes a tyrant. … If one sees murky water gushing from a well... (Also see Flood; Pond; River; Sea; Well)"

Ibn Seerin's Dictionary of Dreams, compiled by Muhammad M. Al-Akili, Pearl Publishing House 1992 (ISBN 1879405032) — "Water" entry, printed p. 469 of the scanned edition.

Al-Nabulsi — ماء (Water) entry

(ماء) هو في المنام حياة طيبة فمن رآه في داره فهو سعادة ومال مجموع وغنيمة وزيادة خير وهو تزويج لقوله تعالى: {وهو الذي خلق من الماء بشراً فجعله نسباً وصهراً}. - (ومن رأى) أن الماء صاف غزير رخص السعر وبسط العدل ومضغ الماء شدة الكد في المعيشة والشرب منه سلامة من العدو وسنة مخصبة لشاربه وإن شرب في النوم من الماء أكثر مما كان يشرب في اليقظة دل على طول عمره. … وقال ابن سيرين رحمه الله تعالى الماء في النوم فتنة في الدين لقوله تعالى: {ماء غدقاً لنفتنهم فيه}. وهو بلاء لقوله تعالى: {إن الله مبتليكم بنهر فمن شرب منه}.

"Water in a dream is a good life. Whoever sees it in his house — happiness, gathered wealth, spoils, increase of good, and marriage, because of the verse 'He created from water a human being and made him lineage and affinity' (Qur'an 25:54). Clear, abundant water: cheap prices and justice spread. Drinking it: safety from the enemy and a fertile year for the drinker; drinking more in sleep than in wakefulness indicates long life. … And Ibn Sirin — may God have mercy on him — said: water in sleep is a trial in religion, per the verse 'water of pouring forth, that We might try them thereby' (18:29), and it is affliction, per 'God is testing you with a river; whoever drinks from it…' (2:249)."

Ta'tir al-Anam fi Ta'bir al-Manam, Abd al-Ghani al-Nabulsi (d. 1731 CE), Dar al-Fikr edition — shamela.ws/book/1217/316 (printed p. 319). Translation from the cited page; the Qur'an references are his, not ours.

Zhou Gong tradition — the water lines

水上行者主大吉 — "Walking on water: great auspiciousness."   水流洋洋有新婚 — "Water flowing broad and full: a new marriage."   饮水不休得大财 — "Drinking water without stopping: great wealth."   流水绕身有狱讼 — "Running water circling the body: lawsuits and imprisonment."   大水澄清大吉祥 — "A great flood that clears: very auspicious."   人家有水见子亡 — "Water in the house: one sees a child perish."   江海涨漫大吉昌 — "Rivers and seas overflowing: great prosperity."   井中沸溢主得财 — "A well boiling over: wealth arrives."

Zhou Gong Jie Meng ("The Dream Book of the Duke of Zhou"), chapter 23 水火盗贼灯烛 (water, fire, thieves, lamps), the standard one-line verdict recension. Attributed to the Duke of Zhou but a later compiled text; translations ours.

Where the books diverge — three real disagreements

The fitna sentence: Al-Nabulsi against his own page

Ninety percent of Al-Nabulsi's water entry is what the Ibn Sirin compilation also carries — good life, marriage, longevity, stagnant water as prison. Then, mid-entry, he inserts "Ibn Sirin said: water is a trial in religion," backed by two Qur'anic proof-texts, and follows it with his own gloss: "whoever is in water is in a trial, affliction and grief." So the same page says water is marriage and fitna. Every English dream site that quotes "water = emotions" from either book skips this sentence entirely, because it doesn't fit the pop-psychology reading. It's the oldest recorded counter-reading of the symbol, and it's right there at shamela.ws/book/1217/316.

Marriage by the verse, sons by the glass

Both Arabic books map water to children and marriage — but through a detail nobody else quotes: the glass. "Whoever is given water in a cup: that is evidence of a child. Whoever drinks clear water from a cup attains good from his child and his wife, because glass is of the substance of women and water is the fetus" (Al-Nabulsi, p. 319; the Ibn Sirin compilation prints the same reasoning — "glass in a dream represents the substance of a woman and water represents a fetus"). A water dream with a vessel in it is, in this system, a conception reading. The Zhou Gong system puts the pregnancy signal elsewhere entirely: 金银杯皿有贵孕 — precious-metal cups and dishes announce a noble pregnancy (chapter 9); 桶盛水者主大吉 — a bucket holding water is very auspicious, 桶无水者主大凶 — an empty bucket, ominous (chapter 11). Same logic — the container is the woman, the water is what's carried — in three literatures that never met.

Flood: prosperity or the child dies

Overflowing water splits the Chinese book against itself and against the Arabic ones. Zhou Gong ch. 23 prints both 江海涨漫大吉昌 (rivers and seas overflowing: great prosperity) and 人家有水见子亡 (water in the household: a child perishes) in the same chapter list — flood is good for the market, lethal inside the home. The Arabic books restrict the positive reading to clear and abundant water lowering prices (both print it, from the same source), and give flood-water in a dwelling an illness reading: seawater running into the house and drunk = sickness; everyone drinking = plague (Ibn Sirin, p. 469).

The thirst layer: what experiment actually showed

Water dreams have one of the few direct experimental tests in dream research. Dement and Wolpert (1958) put volunteers under 24-hour fluid deprivation in a sleep laboratory before REM monitoring: of 15 dream reports collected, 5 contained thirst or drinking content — a third, against a near-zero baseline — though the authors and later reviews (e.g. the intra- and inter-individual analyses collected in the International Journal of Dream Research review of Schredl, 2008) note the confound that deprived people also thought about drinking before sleep. In large single-dreamer series coded with the Hall/Van de Castle system, water appears constantly for some dreamers — the DreamBank "Pegasus" series (a 1,171-dream published corpus) has water in about 22% of dreams — while statistical "typical dream" surveys find water dreams common but not top-ranked like falling or being chased.

What this does and doesn't mean: your body's hydration state can demonstrably put water in a dream without any symbolism at all. That is the modern version of an old Arabic distinction — Al-Nabulsi himself separates drinking-because-thirsty readings from the vision-readings; the classical books assume some water dreams are just thirst and the interpreter's job is to tell which. The research confirms the mechanism exists; it cannot tell you which of your water dreams used it.

This page reports what three classical texts and published research say about water dreams. It is not a prediction, a medical or religious ruling. If a water dream's physical explanation worries you — extreme thirst every night is a medical symptom, see a doctor, not a dream book.
