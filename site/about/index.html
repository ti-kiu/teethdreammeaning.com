About this site

Most dream-meaning pages online repeat the same few folk sentences — "teeth falling out means death" — with no source, no page number, and no way to check. This site started as a refusal of that format.

The method

Every symbol page places the classical sources side by side, each quote with a location you can verify yourself:

  • The English compilation published as Ibn Seerin's Dictionary of Dreams (Muhammad M. Al-Akili, Pearl Publishing House, 1992, ISBN 1879405032), cited to its printed pages and its own end-of-volume index.
  • Al-Nabulsi's Ta'tir al-Anam (Abd al-Ghani al-Nabulsi, d. 1731 CE, Dar al-Fikr edition), cited to the page of its online library edition at Shamela, where the Arabic can be read directly.
  • The received Zhou Gong dream book (周公解梦), where a line for the symbol exists, quoted from the corpus with its chapter — and where no line exists, the page says so plainly instead of inventing one.

No quotation goes on a page before it has been checked character-by-character against the source text. Where the books disagree — and they do — the disagreement stays visible. Where one book carries a clause the other lacks, that too is marked.

What we do not do

We do not predict. We do not diagnose. We do not rule on what God intends for you, and we do not tell you what to do with your money. The full statement is on the disclaimer page. The short version: this site is a reading room for two classical texts and one Chinese tradition, not an oracle.

How the queue works

Symbols are published one per day, in a fixed order worked out in advance: each new page needs its Ibn Sirin entry located in the printed index, its Al-Nabulsi entry located in the Shamela edition, its Zhou Gong line found or its absence verified, and every quotation re-checked against the sources before publication. When a source is unreachable on a given day, the page waits — no page goes up unverified.

Contact

This is a small independent site. Corrections are welcome and are the fastest way to improve it: if you find a quotation that does not match its cited page, or a translation you read differently, that note matters and will be checked against the sources.
